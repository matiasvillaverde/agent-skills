# Company Financial Research

Explain how the company makes money, what its financial evidence shows, what the price
assumes, and what could invalidate the conclusion. Scale the work to the question;
an earnings question does not require a full initiation report or a price target.

## Establish the company and decision

Resolve legal entity, listing/share class and reporting jurisdiction. Distinguish
public stock, private equity, subsidiary and parent. Use the requested date/horizon;
do not mix personal and company portfolio constraints. For a historical question,
only use information available at its cutoff and separate later knowledge.

Start with existing research and the newest relevant original filings, earnings release,
notes and investor materials. Read the sections behind decisive numbers. Use available
structured data to accelerate extraction, reconciling important differences to originals.
The available `finance-data` skill can help with access, periods and normalization;
it is not a required extra stage for a simple question.

## Follow the economics

Choose the few drivers that could change the decision: segments, customer concentration,
pricing, unit economics, margins, reinvestment, working capital, cash conversion,
financing/refinancing needs and competition. Distinguish audited/reported evidence,
management claims, provider estimates and our inference. Headcount, funding and web
traffic are not substitutes for revenue. An absent private-company filing is a gap,
not zero revenue or debt.

Reconcile the relevant income, balance-sheet and cash-flow periods. Preserve currency,
scale, fiscal year, accounting basis, filing date and amendments. Watch cumulative
cash-flow periods, acquisitions, discontinued operations, stock compensation and
dilution. Explain material adjustments rather than silently accepting adjusted EBITDA
or a provider's free-cash-flow label.

For earnings, compare actuals with **dated, pre-release** consensus when available and
with prior company guidance separately. Do not invent consensus or infer a beat from
year-over-year growth. Distinguish the reported quarter, current guidance, analyst
estimates and our forecast; a release can precede the detailed filing.

## Test the valuation and thesis

Use valuation methods appropriate to the business and evidence. Read
[valuation checks](references/valuation.md) for multiples, DCF or scenario work.
Align price date, share count, debt/cash dates, currencies and trailing/forward periods.
Show a range driven by explicit assumptions instead of unsupported target-price precision.
Low multiples can reflect deterioration; a strong company can still be expensive.

Build the strongest supported case for and against the thesis. Investigate disconfirming
results, competitors, accounting quality and financing risks. Name observable evidence
that would change the view. For an update, preserve the prior claim and show what changed;
do not rewrite the original thesis to make every development confirm it.

## Deliver

Lead with the answer, then the decisive business/financial evidence, valuation assumptions,
risks and unresolved questions, with direct dated sources. Include only useful peer tables,
models or charts. Identify whether a conclusion is about business quality, valuation,
solvency or portfolio suitability; these are different claims.

Keep private statements and licensed extracts in the owning private research store.
Research does not place trades, contact companies or change a portfolio. Use an existing
spreadsheet/document skill if the requested deliverable needs one; no fixed report length,
provider subscription or publishing workflow is imposed here.
