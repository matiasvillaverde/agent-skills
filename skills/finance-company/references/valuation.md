# Valuation checks

Use methods that match the economic question; do not produce every model by default.

## Comparable companies

Choose peers by business mix, geography, growth, profitability, capital intensity and
accounting, not ticker proximity. Explain material differences rather than hiding them
in a median. Use the same observation date and comparable fiscal/TTM/forward periods.
Forward estimates need a provider, publication date and clear distinction from actuals.

Market capitalization uses a relevant current share count, not automatically weighted
average diluted shares used in reported EPS. Reconcile material classes, ADR ratios,
convertibles, options and treasury shares. An enterprise-value bridge can start with
equity value plus debt, preferred stock and non-controlling interests, minus available
cash/non-operating investments; lease, pension and restricted-cash treatment depends
on the business and must be consistent with the earnings metric and peers.

Match enterprise value to enterprise operating metrics and equity value to equity
earnings/cash flows. Negative or near-zero earnings can make a multiple meaningless;
mark that explicitly rather than reporting zero or automatically declaring the stock cheap.
Revenue multiples alone do not account for margins, reinvestment or failure risk.

## Discounted cash flow and scenarios

Separate sourced historical values from forecasts. Tie growth, margins, taxes, working
capital and capital spending to the business model. Match currency, inflation basis and
cash-flow claim to the discount rate; do not mix equity cash flow with WACC or enterprise
cash flow with cost of equity. State discounting dates/convention and the EV-to-equity bridge.

Terminal assumptions need a plausible steady state, sustainable reinvestment and a
discount rate above perpetual growth when using that model. Show how much value comes
from the terminal period and sensitivity to the dominant assumptions. Cash balances and
debt used in the bridge need dates and adjustment for material subsequent events.

Use downside/base/upside or a reverse DCF when it helps expose what today's price requires.
Ranges are conditional results, not objective probabilities or guaranteed fair value.
If essential inputs are missing, present the supported range or missing driver instead
of filling the model with plausible-looking numbers.

For banks/insurers, capital adequacy, asset quality, book value and distributable equity
cash flows may matter more than industrial-company EV/EBITDA. For private companies,
round price, preference rights, dilution, liquidity and the valued security matter;
headline post-money valuation is not the realizable value of every share class.
