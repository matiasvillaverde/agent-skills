# Cash and fixed-income product distinctions

| Product | What determines the result | Access and protection checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank current/savings deposit | Rate tiers, plan, variable rate, accrual and fees | Contracting bank, eligible depositor, aggregate scheme limit, withdrawal conditions and operational access |
| Term deposit | Fixed term/rate and actual day-count | Early termination may be unavailable or forfeit interest; renewals may be automatic; scheme and legal bank still matter |
| Individual government bill/bond | Purchase/dirty price, coupon/discount, maturity proceeds, issuer repayment, dealing/FX costs | Early-sale price and spread can differ from maturity value. Sovereign credit and custody risks remain; no deposit insurance |
| Government money-market fund | Portfolio income, exact share class, embedded fund fees and additional platform costs | Public-debt fund differs from bank/corporate-credit MMF. Read NAV structure, dealing cutoffs, settlement and liquidity-management terms; no deposit insurance or guaranteed capital |
| Bond ETF/fund | Portfolio yield, duration, credit, rolling holdings and costs | The portfolio generally does not mature on one date or promise return of the purchase amount. Exchange trading and underlying asset liquidity differ |
| Inflation-linked bond | Defined inflation index/lag, real yield, coupon, price and any contractual floor | Protects according to its own index and maturity terms, not every investor's cost of living. Can lose market value when real yields rise |

For a cash-management recommendation, compare a small set of feasible candidates on a
consistent date and amount. A useful row includes issuer/legal entity, product and ISIN
when applicable, currency/hedging, eligible owner, rate/yield convention and date,
incremental costs, withdrawal/settlement, protection and principal risks. Leave uncertain
fields explicit rather than filling them from a similar product or another country page.

For funds, inspect the exact accumulation/distribution and institutional/retail class.
Trading currency alone does not establish portfolio currency or currency hedging. A
seven-day annualized yield, distribution yield and portfolio yield to maturity answer
different questions. Confirm whether displayed yields are already after fund expenses.

For bonds, coupon rate is not yield to maturity. Account for accrued interest on purchase
where relevant. Sovereign auction results and interpolated yield curves provide context;
the specific security's available bid/ask and costs govern an actual purchase. A high
coupon bond bought above par can have a modest or negative holding-period result.

For guarantees, read the [EU deposit-scheme overview](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/banking/banking-regulation/deposit-guarantee-schemes_en)
and the applicable national scheme/bank disclosure when evaluating EU deposits. Never
apply an EU limit to a different jurisdiction or an investment product merely because
the provider also offers banking. Protection does not imply uninterrupted payments.

For interest-rate sensitivity, [FINRA explains duration](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/bonds-interest-rate-changes-duration).
For US bills, notes, bonds and TIPS, use [TreasuryDirect's terms](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/)
while verifying the investor's actual account/broker access separately. Country-specific
retail access, minimums and tax descriptions do not automatically apply to a foreign company.

Show the scenario that reverses the ranking: falling floating rates, early cash need,
adverse FX, a required higher subscription, loss of an introductory rate, or a product
the company cannot open. A useful recommendation remains explicit about those conditions.
