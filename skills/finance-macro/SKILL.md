# Macro and Cross-Market Research

Build a dated explanation of the environment and its implications for the user's
decision. Distinguish observed conditions, market pricing and our forecast.

Infer the relevant economy, base currency, horizon and portfolio/business exposure.
Choose only the indicators that could change the answer. Use existing research first,
then available market data and original central-bank/statistical releases. The
`finance-data` skill, when available, contains source and vintage guidance.

## Make the snapshot comparable

Record observation and release dates, units, session/timezone, source and revisions.
Align equity closes, futures, FX, commodities and continuously traded assets before
comparing moves. An ETF is not the index; a futures price is not a government-bond yield;
a daily bar may be incomplete. Preserve futures contract/roll and adjustment details.

For economic data, distinguish month-on-month, year-on-year and annualized growth,
nominal/real values and seasonal adjustment. Read the release methodology and revisions
where material. A reference-period value released tomorrow cannot explain what was
known today. Use historical vintages for historical decisions rather than the latest
revised series. Treat consensus and market-implied expectations as sourced estimates.

## Explain and challenge

Follow channels from policy/inflation/growth through yields, credit, FX, profits and
valuation. Check alternatives and leading/lagging timing; correlation alone does not
prove the mechanism. Disaggregate when a headline aggregate hides a decisive difference.

For downturn questions, distinguish demand-led recession/disinflation, supply/energy
inflation, liquidity/credit stress and a valuation repricing without recession. Compare
what each would imply, and what evidence supports or weakens it. Avoid treating a yield
curve, VIX level, falling stock index or single weak release as a crash probability.
Policy-rate cuts do not guarantee falling long-term bond yields; fiscal/credit/term
premia and inflation expectations can move differently.

Separate nominal capital, purchasing power, drawdown tolerance and payment access.
Currency matching and duration can matter more than a headline yield. Explain how a
proposed hedge could fail under another plausible shock. Historical safe-haven behavior
is evidence to examine, not a guarantee of the next event.

## Deliver

Lead with a supported view and the few dated observations that drive it. Use a compact
scenario table when outcomes differ materially. Label illustrative shocks and assumptions;
do not assign probabilities without a defensible method. Name the releases or conditions
that would change the view and material access/coverage gaps.

Match recommendations to established cash needs and risk capacity; do not silently turn
an outlook into an allocation or order. For cash-parking comparisons, use `finance-treasury`
when available. Keep a durable source/claim trail for recurring research, with private
holdings and licensed data outside public reports.
